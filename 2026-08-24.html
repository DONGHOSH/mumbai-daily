Parliament passed the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026, transferring authority over mineral extraction and management from state governments to the Centre. Mineral-rich states, particularly Odisha, fear both a significant erosion of their revenue base and a weakening of India's federal structure.

HIGH IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS HIGH IMPACT
▼Odisha State Government
Royalties and mineral revenues that anchor Odisha's state budget now fall under Central control, directly threatening its fiscal independence and development spending.
▼Tribal and Mining Communities in Odisha, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh
Centralised licensing decisions bypass state-level community consent mechanisms, raising displacement risks and weakening protections under PESA and Forest Rights Act.
◆Mining and Metals Sector
Companies now negotiate leases and approvals with a single Central authority, potentially accelerating clearances but concentrating regulatory risk in one tier of government.
▼Opposition-ruled Mineral-rich States
The amendment sets a precedent for Centre to override state jurisdiction over natural resources, intensifying federal tensions and coalition politics around resource-sharing.
