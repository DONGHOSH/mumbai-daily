India Clarifies Law on Migrant Pushbacks, Detention and Deportation

India's legal framework distinguishes between pushbacks, detention, and deportation of undocumented migrants from Bangladesh and Myanmar, with pushbacks — summary border returns without formal proceedings — occupying a contested grey zone under domestic and international law. The distinction matters because each mechanism carries different legal obligations, rights of the individual, and consequences for the state.

HIGH IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS HIGH IMPACT
▼Undocumented Migrants from Bangladesh and Myanmar
Pushbacks bypass formal asylum or refugee hearings, exposing migrants to removal without any legal recourse or protection assessment.
▼Border State Governments (Assam, West Bengal, Tripura, Manipur)
States bear frontline enforcement responsibility but face legal exposure if pushback operations are challenged in courts as unconstitutional or violating non-refoulement norms.
◆Union Home Ministry
Policy ambiguity on pushback legality forces the Centre to balance domestic political pressure on immigration with India's bilateral obligations to Dhaka and Naypyidaw.
◆Human Rights and Legal Aid Organisations
Lack of a codified pushback statute creates litigation opportunities to challenge summary removals before High Courts and the Supreme Court on due-process grounds.

Kunal Shah, founder of fintech startup CRED and co-founder of FreeCharge, has been appointed global head of WhatsApp. Shah is among India's most prominent tech entrepreneurs and a prolific angel investor in the domestic startup ecosystem.

Lenders Look Beyond CIBIL Score When Approving Loans

Banks and NBFCs now weigh income stability, repayment behaviour, and credit utilisation alongside credit scores when deciding loan eligibility. A strong CIBIL number alone no longer guarantees approval.

LOW IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS LOW IMPACT
▼Retail Loan Applicants
Borrowers with decent CIBIL scores risk rejection if income is irregular or credit utilisation remains high, making financial discipline more critical than a single number.
◆First-Time Borrowers
Those with thin credit histories must now demonstrate stable income and responsible credit behaviour to build a complete lender-ready financial profile.
