Fitch Says Indian Banks Can Absorb ECL Provisioning Shift

Fitch Ratings has assessed Indian banks as adequately capitalised to manage the transition to expected credit loss provisioning. The shift is projected to reduce the banking system's average CET1 ratio by 30 basis points in FY2027-28.

MEDIUM IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS MEDIUM IMPACT
▼PSU Banks
State-owned lenders, with structurally thinner capital buffers than private peers, will face closer scrutiny as the 30 bps CET1 compression lands in FY28.
▲RBI
Fitch's benign assessment validates the regulator's phased ECL rollout strategy, reducing pressure to delay or water down the framework further.
▼SME and Corporate Borrowers
Forward-looking ECL provisioning will push banks to price credit risk more conservatively upfront, potentially tightening loan terms for higher-risk borrowers.

Zerodha MTF Book Hits Rs 7,400 Crore as Retail Leverage Surges

India's retail traders have rapidly shifted from cash market activity to leveraged positions since the pandemic, with Zerodha's Margin Trading Facility book swelling to nearly Rs 7,400 crore. Combined brokerage costs and interest charges on MTF positions can quietly erode returns, raising risk concerns for retail participants.

MEDIUM IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS MEDIUM IMPACT
▼Retail MTF Traders
Rising interest rates plus brokerage fees compound the cost of holding leveraged MTF positions, shrinking net returns and amplifying downside risk in volatile markets.
▲Zerodha
A Rs 7,400 crore MTF book generates significant and recurring interest income, reducing dependence on transaction-fee revenue that SEBI's F&O curbs have squeezed.
◆SEBI
Rapid retail leverage build-up in MTF books across brokers raises systemic risk flags, likely intensifying regulatory scrutiny of margin lending norms and client-level exposure limits.
