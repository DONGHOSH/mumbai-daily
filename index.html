SC rules approved insolvency plans binding, blocks creditor exits

The Supreme Court has held that under the IBC, neither the Committee of Creditors nor resolution applicants can withdraw from an approved resolution plan on the grounds that changed financial conditions have made the deal unattractive. The ruling eliminates a contested exit route that parties had sought to use when debtor finances improved after plan approval.

MEDIUM IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS MEDIUM IMPACT
▼Resolution Applicants
Bidders who win stressed-asset auctions can no longer back out post-approval by citing improved debtor finances; committed capital and obligations are locked in.
▼Committee of Creditors — Banks and NBFCs
Creditor banks must honour agreed haircuts and plan terms even if the debtor's recovery makes the original deal less favourable than current alternatives.
▲Stressed Companies and Their Employees
Binding plan execution reduces the risk of deals collapsing mid-process, providing greater certainty for operational continuity, vendor payments, and job retention.

High Credit Score Alone Does Not Guarantee Loan Approval

A CIBIL score above 750 does not automatically secure a loan, as lenders weigh multiple eligibility factors beyond the score. Checking one's own credit score does not reduce it, and salary levels have no direct bearing on the score itself.
