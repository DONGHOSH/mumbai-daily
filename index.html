RBI guidelines permit home loan borrowers facing repayment stress to restructure their loans by reducing EMIs or extending tenure. The relief is not free — total interest outgo rises and the borrower's credit score is negatively marked by bureaus.

Restructured retail accounts require higher provisioning under RBI norms, quietly pressuring lenders' reported asset quality and margins.
▼Borrowers Seeking Future Credit
A restructuring flag on credit history shrinks eligibility for top-up loans, personal loans, or refinancing at competitive rates for years ahead.

Credit Bureaus Take 30-45 Days to Reflect a Closed Personal Loan

Repaying a personal loan does not trigger an immediate credit score improvement because bureaus typically take 30-45 days to update records. Closing a loan also alters a borrower's credit mix, which can temporarily depress the score even after full repayment.

Credit Cards Levy Up to 3.5% Markup on Every Foreign Transaction

Credit card users making overseas or foreign-currency purchases face layered charges — forex markup fees of 1–3.5%, GST on those fees, and dynamic currency conversion risks — that can push the final bill well above the displayed price. Cardholders unaware of these mechanics routinely overpay on international travel and cross-border online purchases.

MEDIUM IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS MEDIUM IMPACT
▼Overseas Travellers
Stacking of forex markup, GST, and dynamic currency conversion can add 5–7% to every swipe abroad, making low-markup or zero-markup travel cards materially cheaper.
▼Cross-border Online Shoppers
Indians buying from foreign websites face the same markup structure without leaving home, silently inflating bills on every international e-commerce or subscription purchase.
▼Credit Card Issuers
Growing consumer awareness of forex markup revenue may accelerate a shift toward zero-markup travel cards, compressing a reliable fee-income stream for standard card portfolios.
