Indian banks are projected to see credit growth moderate to 12-13% in FY27, with deposit growth expected to stabilise at a matching pace. The shift away from low-cost savings deposits continues to pressure bank funding structures.

IMPACT ANALYSIS MEDIUM IMPACT
▼Retail Borrowers
Moderating credit growth signals banks will tighten lending pipelines, slowing approvals for home, auto and personal loans.
▼MSMEs
A slower credit expansion cycle restricts access to working capital and growth financing for small businesses most dependent on bank loans.
▼Banks (PSU & Private)
Declining low-cost CASA deposits force banks to raise deposit rates, compressing net interest margins even as loan book growth decelerates.

Bank Credit Grows 16%, Outpaces Deposit Growth

Bank credit accelerated to 16% growth in the fortnight ending April 30, well ahead of deposit growth at 12.3%, pushing outstanding credit to Rs 212.12 lakh crore. The credit-deposit ratio rose to 82.01%, signalling that lending is expanding faster than the deposit base can keep pace.

MEDIUM IMPACT
IMPACT ANALYSIS MEDIUM IMPACT
▲Retail and Corporate Borrowers
Accelerating credit growth reflects banks actively deploying capital, keeping loan availability healthy for home, auto, and business borrowers.
▲FD Holders and Depositors
With credit outrunning deposits, banks face funding pressure and are likely to raise fixed deposit rates to attract fresh liabilities.
▼RBI and Bank Regulators
A CD ratio of 82.01% — with credit growing 370 basis points faster than deposits — warrants monitoring for systemic liquidity stress in the banking system.
